MEP Procurement — Report Walkthrough

Executive walkthrough of the MEP Procurement report running in Power BI Desktop. It follows the questions a procurement manager at a UAE MEP contractor asks every week: what have we committed, what did negotiation save, where are we paying above market as LME copper rises, which suppliers deliver late or fail inspection, and how clean the PR-to-PO process is. The video slices the same report by year, project and discipline, then uses the AI Decomposition Tree to trace spend back to one supplier on one project.

What the Walkthrough Shows, Step by Step

Every number below is read straight off the report in the video. The headline sentence at the top of each page is a DAX measure, so it rewrites itself every time a slicer changes.

0:00

Executive overview, 2026 year to date

With the Year slicer on 2026, the report shows AED 442.9M committed on 1,251 purchase orders with 26 suppliers, up 68% on the same months of 2025. Negotiation saved AED 17.2M (3.7%) against first quotes, but the price we paid ran +1.7% above the market benchmark, and only 28.9% of spend sat on framework agreements. The headline calls out the biggest leak straight away: Chillers cost AED 6.0M above market, and the weakest supplier on delivery was Pearl Valves Trading at 54% on time.

  • Monthly spend and savings rate — combo chart, spend columns against the negotiated-savings line, so a month with heavy buying but thin savings stands out.
  • Spend by discipline — Electrical and HVAC dominate, driven by cables, switchgear, chillers and generators.
  • Spend vs budget by project — actual PO value against the same quantities at budget rates frozen at each project’s start.
  • Where we paid above market — price variance by category, only categories above benchmark: Chillers, Generators, Switchgear, Fire alarm.
0:08

Drill into one project: Creekside Residences Tower

Picking a single project re-scopes every visual. Creekside committed AED 71.3M in 2026, saved AED 2.8M (3.8%), paid +2.3% above market — worse than the portfolio — and achieved 81.4% on-time delivery. The headline now names a different weak supplier for this project (Emirates Ductwork Industries), which is exactly the conversation a project procurement lead needs to have.

0:16

Project + discipline: Creekside fire fighting package

Adding the Discipline slicer narrows to one trade package: AED 4.3M on 22 POs with 4 suppliers. Delivery is strong at 95.2% on time, but price is not: +3.4% above market, and the headline isolates Fire alarm devices at AED 0.2M above market. Hovering the donut shows Fire fighting as 100% of the selection, confirming the filter context.

0:31

Supplier performance, full period

All years: 26 active suppliers, 78.8% on-time delivery, late deliveries average 9.0 days, a 5.3% non-conformance (NCR) rate, 89.5% of invoices passing the PO / GRN / invoice 3-way match first time, and 6 open POs already past their need-by date. The weakest on delivery across the whole period is Hanzhou HVAC Export Co at 53% on time.

  • Delivery vs price by supplier — a bubble per supplier: on-time % across, price vs market up, bubble size = spend. Bottom-right is fast and cheap; top-left needs action.
  • Supplier concentration (Pareto) — spend by supplier with a cumulative-share line built with ALLSELECTED, showing how few suppliers carry most of the spend.
  • Supplier scorecard — one row per supplier with origin (Local / GCC / Import), ICV certification and a weighted score.
0:55

One project’s suppliers: JAFZA Logistics Hub

In 2026, JAFZA committed AED 37.3M with 25 suppliers: 85.4% on time and late deliveries only 5.1 days late on average, but an 8.5% NCR rate — well above the 5.3% portfolio average — so the issue on this site is quality, not speed. Narrowing further to its fire fighting package shows 4 suppliers at 92.9% on time, a 100% first-pass invoice match, and Fire alarm again AED 0.1M above market.

1:11

Process and compliance

Requisition to purchase order takes 12.1 days on average. 79.3% of spend went through a framework agreement or competitive tender; 3.6% was maverick spend (a PO raised before an approved requisition); 12.9% was single source and 7.8% spot buys; tenders drew 3.7 bids on average. Switching to 2026 shows the cycle-time ranking by buyer: Sami Haddad at 14.7 days against Aisha Rahman at 9.9 — a five-day gap on the same process that is worth a workload or approval-routing review.

  • Compliance trend — maverick and single-source share of spend month by month, so a spike after a site emergency is visible immediately.
1:32

AI Decomposition Tree: where the money actually goes

Starting from total 2026 spend, the tree splits by Supplier (largest: Al Wasl Cables, AED 31.8M), then by Project (Creekside Residences Tower, AED 3.7M of that), then by Discipline (Electrical). In three clicks a manager goes from a portfolio number to one supplier’s cable orders on one site — the starting point for a volume-rebate or framework renegotiation.

Findings a Procurement Manager Would Act On

Renegotiate chillers.

Chillers are the single largest overpayment: AED 10.7M above benchmark over the period (+7.7%), concentrated in one imported supplier priced well above local alternatives.

Hedge copper-linked items.

LV cable prices tracked LME copper up through 2026. Budgets priced at project start now show overruns even where the buying itself was below market — a case for copper-indexed framework pricing.

Review two supplier contracts.

Hanzhou HVAC Export Co (53% on time, 13.5% NCR) and Coolline Trading LLC (63% on time) score below 60 and are flagged “Review contract”.

Move spend onto frameworks.

Only about 30% of spend is on framework agreements. High-volume commodities (cable, pipe, containment, ductwork) are the natural candidates.

Close the buyer cycle-time gap.

A five-day spread in PR-to-PO time between buyers on the same process points to approval routing or workload, not market conditions.

Fix quality at JAFZA.

An 8.5% NCR rate at one site against 5.3% overall means inspection and supplier qualification on that project need attention.

KPI Definitions

The procurement KPIs used on the report and exactly how each is calculated.

Committed spendSum of PO value issued in the period.
Negotiated savingsFirst quote minus final PO value; savings % divides by quoted value.
Price vs market (PPV)PO value minus the benchmark rate in the PO month × quantity. Positive = paid above market.
Variance to budgetOrdered quantity at budget rates (frozen at project start) minus PO value.
On-time deliveryDelivered POs received on or before the need-by date, over all delivered POs.
Avg days lateAverage delay of late deliveries only.
NCR rateDeliveries with a non-conformance report, over delivered POs.
First-pass invoice matchInvoices passing the PO / GRN / invoice 3-way match first time.
PR to PO cycleDays from approved requisition to purchase order.
Maverick spendSpend with no approved requisition before the PO was raised.
Competitive spendSpend under a framework agreement or competitive tender.
Supplier score40% on-time + 30% quality + 30% price vs market, on a 0–100 scale.

Automated Narrative Headline

Finds the category with the largest overpayment and the least reliable supplier (15+ deliveries), then writes the sentence that opens every page. This is what changes in the video every time a slicer moves.

Headline =
VAR cats = ADDCOLUMNS ( VALUES ( Items[Category] ), "@ppv", [PPV] )
VAR topCat = TOPN ( 1, cats, [@ppv], DESC )
VAR supps =
    FILTER (
        ADDCOLUMNS ( VALUES ( Suppliers[Supplier] ),
            "@d", [Delivered POs], "@otd", [On-time delivery %] ),
        [@d] >= 15
    )
VAR worst = TOPN ( 1, supps, [@otd], ASC )
RETURN
    "Negotiation saved AED " & FORMAT ( [Negotiated savings] / 1000000, "#,0.0" ) & "M ("
        & FORMAT ( [Savings %], "0.0%" ) & "), but " & MAXX ( topCat, Items[Category] )
        & " cost AED " & FORMAT ( MAXX ( topCat, [@ppv] ) / 1000000, "#,0.0" ) & "M above market."
        & " Weakest on delivery: " & MAXX ( worst, Suppliers[Supplier] )
        & " at " & FORMAT ( MAXX ( worst, [@otd] ), "0%" ) & " on time."

Purchase Price Variance vs Market

Separates market inflation from buying performance: each PO is compared with the benchmark rate in its own month, and copper-linked items move with LME copper.

PPV = [Spend] - [Benchmark value]

PPV % = DIVIDE ( [PPV], [Benchmark value] )

PPV above market AED M = IF ( [PPV] > 0, [PPV] / 1000000 )

Weighted Supplier Score and Action

One number per supplier from delivery, quality and price, then turned into a recommendation for the category manager.

Supplier score =
VAR otd = [On-time delivery %]
VAR quality = MAX ( 0, 1 - 5 * [NCR rate] )
VAR price = MIN ( 1, MAX ( 0, 0.5 - 5 * [PPV %] ) )
RETURN
    IF ( NOT ISBLANK ( otd ),
        100 * ( 0.4 * otd + 0.3 * quality + 0.3 * price ) )

Supplier action =
VAR s = [Supplier score]
RETURN
    SWITCH ( TRUE (),
        ISBLANK ( s ), BLANK (),
        s < 60, "Review contract",
        s < 72, "Improvement plan",
        "Preferred" )

Supplier Pareto (Cumulative Share)

Running share of spend, largest supplier first, respecting every slicer through ALLSELECTED.

Cumulative spend % =
VAR thisSpend = [Spend]
VAR allSupp = ALLSELECTED ( Suppliers[Supplier] )
RETURN
    DIVIDE (
        SUMX ( FILTER ( allSupp, [Spend] >= thisSpend ), [Spend] ),
        CALCULATE ( [Spend], allSupp )
    )

Like-for-like Prior Year (the “up 68%” in the summary)

Shifts any month selection back 12 months with TREATAS, and only compares months that have a prior-year month.

Spend PY =
VAR months = VALUES ( 'Calendar'[Month Number] )
RETURN
    CALCULATE ( [Spend], REMOVEFILTERS ( 'Calendar' ),
        TREATAS ( SELECTCOLUMNS ( months, "m", 'Calendar'[Month Number] - 12 ),
            'Calendar'[Month Number] ) )

Spend comparable =
SUMX ( FILTER ( VALUES ( 'Calendar'[Month Number] ), 'Calendar'[Month Number] > 12 ), [Spend] )

Spend YoY % = DIVIDE ( [Spend comparable] - [Spend PY], [Spend PY] )

Maverick Spend and PR-to-PO Cycle

The two process KPIs on the compliance page.

Maverick spend % =
DIVIDE (
    CALCULATE ( [Spend], 'Purchase Orders'[PR Raised] = "No" ),
    [Spend]
)

Avg PR to PO days =
CALCULATE (
    AVERAGE ( 'Purchase Orders'[PR to PO Days] ),
    'Purchase Orders'[PR Raised] = "Yes"
)

Automated Narrative Headline

Identifies the highest-growth channel and largest promotion dependency, writing the insight as plain English above the KPIs.

Headline =
VAR t = ADDCOLUMNS ( VALUES ( Channels[Channel] ), "@g", [Net sales growth], "@p", [Promo share] )
VAR valid = FILTER ( t, NOT ISBLANK ( [@g] ) )
VAR fast = TOPN ( 1, valid, [@g], DESC )
VAR promo = TOPN ( 1, t, [@p], DESC )
VAR promoText = MAXX ( promo, Channels[Channel] ) & " relies most on promotions, at " & FORMAT ( MAXX ( promo, [@p] ), "0%" ) & " of sales."
RETURN
    IF (
        NOT ISEMPTY ( fast ),
        MAXX ( fast, Channels[Channel] ) & " is the fastest-growing channel, up "
            & FORMAT ( MAXX ( fast, [@g] ), "0%" ) & " on the year before, while " & promoText,
        "Select a channel or year to view performance drivers."
    )

Core DIFC Fund House DAX Measures

Asset management analytics powering time-weighted compound returns, liquidity stress tests, and automated redemption alerts.

Compounded Return (PRODUCTX)

Calculates true asset-weighted geometric compound returns across dynamically selected multi-month holding periods.

Return =
VAR selMonths = VALUES ( 'Fund Monthly'[Date] )
RETURN
    IF ( NOT ISEMPTY ( selMonths ), PRODUCTX ( selMonths, 1 + [Monthly return] ) - 1 )

Growth of $100 vs Benchmark

Indexes initial capital to $100 and tracks cumulative returns over time against the benchmark index.

Growth of 100 =
VAR cur = MAX ( 'Calendar'[Date] )
VAR monthsSoFar = FILTER ( ALLSELECTED ( 'Calendar' ), 'Calendar'[Date] <= cur )
RETURN
    IF (
        NOT ISEMPTY ( VALUES ( 'Fund Monthly'[Date] ) ),
        100 * PRODUCTX ( monthsSoFar, 1 + [Monthly return] )
    )

Liquidity Payable Within 7 Days (%)

Computes immediate capital redemption capability by evaluating dealing terms payable on Next Day or within 2 to 7 business days.

Payable within 7 days =
CALCULATE ( [Share of assets payable], Liquidity[Bucket Order] <= 2 )

Client Redemption Alert (>20% Outflow)

Identifies high-value institutional accounts who reduced their fund holding by more than 20% in the trailing 12 months.

Clients cutting over 20 percent =
COUNTROWS (
    FILTER (
        Clients,
        Clients[Held Sep 2025] > 0
            && Clients[Held Sep 2026] < Clients[Held Sep 2025] * 0.8
    )
)

Automated Smart Narrative Headline

Evaluates community yield outliers and automatically generates dynamic English insights above the KPIs.

Headline =
VAR t = ADDCOLUMNS ( VALUES ( Areas[Area] ), "@y", [Average gross yield], "@n", [Transactions] )
VAR valid = FILTER ( t, [@n] >= 10 )
VAR n = COUNTROWS ( valid )
VAR hi = TOPN ( 1, valid, [@y], DESC )
VAR lo = TOPN ( 1, valid, [@y], ASC )
VAR hiName = MAXX ( hi, Areas[Area] )
VAR hiY = MAXX ( hi, [@y] )
VAR loName = MAXX ( lo, Areas[Area] )
VAR loY = MAXX ( lo, [@y] )
RETURN
    SWITCH (
        TRUE (),
        n = 0, "Not enough sales in this selection to compare communities.",
        n = 1, hiName & " sold at AED " & FORMAT ( [Price per sq ft], "#,0" ) & " per sq ft with a " & FORMAT ( hiY, "0.0%" ) & " gross yield.",
        hiName & " pays the most rent for the price at a " & FORMAT ( hiY, "0.0%" ) & " gross yield, "
            & FORMAT ( ( hiY - loY ) * 100, "0.0" ) & " points above " & loName & "."
    )

Cash on Delivery Return Rate (%)

Isolates return frequency specifically for orders settled via cash at the doorstep.

COD return rate =
DIVIDE (
    SUM ( 'Sales Orders'[COD Return Count] ),
    SUM ( 'Sales Orders'[COD Order Count] )
)

Prepaid Return Rate (%)

Measures return performance for credit card, debit card, and buy-now-pay-later digital transactions.

Prepaid return rate =
DIVIDE (
    SUM ( 'Sales Orders'[Return Count] ) - SUM ( 'Sales Orders'[COD Return Count] ),
    [Orders] - SUM ( 'Sales Orders'[COD Order Count] )
)
